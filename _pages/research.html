Most of my work comes back to one question: how should a manipulation task be described so that a robot can decide how to grasp an object and how to move it? My answer has been to describe the task by the motion the object has to go through, as a constant screw motion or a sequence of them. By Chasles' theorem, any rigid body motion can be approximated arbitrarily closely by such a sequence, so the same description covers opening a drawer, turning a knob, pivoting a box, pouring and scooping. I use it to judge whether a grasp can produce the motion, to find grasps from point clouds, and to plan the robot's motion, often from a single kinesthetic demonstration.

  1. 1 Describe the task as a sequence of constant screw motions of the object
  2. 2 Score grasps by whether they can produce that motion
  3. 3 Find grasps from point clouds, and regrasp when one grasp isn't enough
  4. 4 Plan the motion with screw linear interpolation, from demonstrations and with contacts
